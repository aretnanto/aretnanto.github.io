Perception without sensation

Prototyping touch for visual analytics with pseudo-haptics, on the computer everyone already has.

Aditya Retnanto · November 4, 2026

Notes

I want to add touch to visual analytics without new hardware. The way it works says something about how much the field relies on vision.

Motivation

Visual analytics runs on vision

Visualization is “the use of computer-supported, interactive, visual representations of data to amplify cognition.”
Card, Mackinlay & Shneiderman 1999

Visual analytics is “the science of analytical reasoning facilitated by interactive visual interfaces.”
Thomas & Cook 2005

  • The most common setting is still a desktop with a mouse and keyboard.
  • The hand only sends input.
  • The data and the feedback from every action come back through the eyes.
Notes

Both definitions keep the word I want to question, visual. Interaction is central to visual analytics, but on the desktop it only flows one way.

Theory

Sensation is not perception

Sensation

The signal

What the receptors register. Light on the retina, pressure on the skin, the stretch of a muscle.

Perception

The interpretation

What the brain concludes from those signals, using context and expectation.

  • Helmholtz called perception unconscious inference. The brain guesses the most likely cause of what it senses.
  • Gregory described perceptions as hypotheses. Illusions are the cases where the guess is wrong.
  • So the same sensation can produce different perceptions, and different sensations can produce the same one.

Helmholtz 1867 · Gregory 1980 · Goldstein, Sensation and Perception

Notes

Ask the room which line is longer. Then point out they are the same length. That is the gap between sensation and perception, and everything in this project lives in it.

Theory

When senses disagree, vision usually wins

  • Rock & Victor showed people a square through a lens that distorted it. They felt the shape they saw.
  • Seeing a rubber hand stroked shifts where people feel their own hand.
  • Ernst & Banks explained why. The brain weights each sense by how reliable it is, and vision is usually the most precise.

Rock & Victor 1964 · Botvinick & Cohen 1998 · Ernst & Banks 2002

Notes

Drag the slider. When vision is precise, the combined estimate sits on the visual value. Blur vision and touch takes over. Pseudo-haptics works because on a screen the cursor is precise and the hand’s sense of its own movement is not.

Theory

What visualization assumed

The foundation

  • Bertin’s visual variables and Cleveland & McGill’s graphical perception measure how accurately people decode a visual signal.
  • They assume perception follows the stimulus. A longer bar looks longer, with some error.

The cracks

  • Many perceptual “facts” in visualization rest on thin evidence (Kosara 2016).
  • People compare charts through shortcuts such as average length, and these can be fooled on purpose (Jardine et al. 2020, Ondov et al. 2020).

Pseudo-haptics is the extreme case. Perception changes while sensation stays the same.

Bertin 1967 · Cleveland & McGill 1984 · Kosara 2016 · Jardine et al. 2020 · Ondov et al. 2020

Live demo

Feel it yourself

Click the chart and move across the bars. Taller bars slow the cursor down.

  • Hide bars. Can you still find the tallest one?
  • Hide cursor. The feeling should disappear, because the mouse itself never changes.

Esc releases the pointer.

Lécuyer et al. 2004 · Lécuyer 2009

Notes

Let someone try it. The hand moves the same everywhere. Only what the eye sees changes. That is perception without sensation.

Motivation

An apparent paradox

The design goal

Lower cognitive load

Visualization moves work from thinking to seeing.

The capability

Load vision more

Pseudo-haptics adds a touch channel by putting more work on vision, the sense we usually add touch to relieve.

  • Separate senses usually draw on separate resources, which is why touch normally helps (Wickens 2008).
  • Pseudo-haptic touch arrives through vision, so it may not get that benefit.
  • But perceptual load is not cognitive load (Lavie et al. 2004). Extra seeing does not have to mean extra thinking.
Everyday example

Subtitles

Many viewers prefer reading subtitles to hearing a dub. They take on more visual work to avoid a mismatch between lips and voice, and to keep the original.

Notes

It only looks like a paradox from visualization’s side. Whether the extra load is perceptual and cheap, or cognitive and costly, is a question this tool can study.

Motivation

Touch costs time and hardware

  • Physicalization encodes data in objects people can touch (Jansen et al. 2015).
  • They are usually 3D printed, and can even sense touch (Bae et al. 2022, 2023).
  • A print takes from minutes to overnight, so designers iterate about once a day (Mueller et al. 2014).
  • Almost everyone has a computer and a mouse. Few people have a printer.

Pseudo-haptics is a low-fidelity version of the touch that fabrication provides. It renders instantly, changes with the data, and needs only a mouse.

Follmer et al. 2013 · Abtahi & Follmer 2018 · Mueller et al. 2014

The project

A prototyping tool for felt data

A browser tool where designers map data to a feel, test whether it comes across without vision, and decide what is worth fabricating.

Understand

What touch can carry

Measure which felt differences people tell apart, and turn them into design requirements such as tactile palettes, before anything is printed.

Realize

A new display medium

The cursor itself becomes a way to display data through touch on a common computer.

Users

  • Visualization and physicalization designers and researchers

Analytical tasks

  • Find a pattern by feel
  • Separate significant structure from noise
  • Reshape data and review how it changed
  • Compare the same data on screen and in print

Course directions · new visual analytics approach, and advanced analysis environments (multimodal interface)

Live · find a pattern by feel

Press Mystery pattern, feel the grid with the colours hidden, then reveal. Try Hide cursor for the control.

Notes

This is the analytical task in its simplest form. Known patterns mean we know the right answer.

Live · feel the peaks that matter

Maunga Whau volcano. Raise Keep peaks more prominent than and the small peaks flatten, in what you feel too. Computed with persistent homology.

Notes

Computation decides what is felt. Each peak is ranked by how far it rises above the saddle that joins it to a higher peak, and peaks below the threshold are flattened.

Live · reshape the data, then replay

Hold the button and push sand into a hill. The heavier the load, the slower it moves. Switch Show to see where you worked, then press Replay.

Notes

Every brush step is recorded, so the session can be rebuilt exactly. This is the provenance side of visual analytics, applied to a felt surface.

What it can express

One set of channels and controls produces every prototype.

Technical checks

Does the feel match the data?

Scripted cursor sweeps compare the felt signal with the true values. Replay must rebuild a session exactly.

Usage scenarios

Herman et al.’s tasks

Trace a path downhill, compare two heights, find the extremes, felt on Maunga Whau.

No study with participants, so no human-subjects review is needed. Toolkit evaluation strategies, Ledo et al. 2018.
