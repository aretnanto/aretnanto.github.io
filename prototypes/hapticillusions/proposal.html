Perception without sensation

Prototyping touch for visualization with pseudo-haptics, on the computer everyone already has.

Aditya Retnanto · November 4, 2026

Notes

One sentence. I want to add touch to visualization without new hardware, and the way I do it says something about how much visualization relies on vision.

Motivation

Visualization runs on vision

Visualization is “the use of computer-supported, interactive, visual representations of data to amplify cognition.”

  • The most common setting is still a desktop with a mouse and keyboard.
  • The hand only sends input.
  • The data and the feedback from every action come back through the eyes.

Card, Mackinlay & Shneiderman 1999 · Shneiderman 1983 (direct manipulation)

Notes

Card’s definition keeps “amplify cognition” and “interactive”. The word I want to question is “visual”. On the desktop one sense carries both the data and the result of everything we do.

Motivation

Physicalization adds touch, at a cost

What it adds

Data you can hold

Physical objects encode data in their shape and material, so touch can carry it.

Jansen et al. 2015

How it is made

Usually 3D printed

Prints can even sense touch, for example networks you select by touching them.

Bae et al. 2022, 2023

The cost

Slow and scarce

A print takes from minutes to overnight, so designers iterate about once a day. Few people own a printer.

Mueller et al. 2014

Almost everyone has a computer and a mouse. Far fewer people can fabricate an object, and nobody can reprint it every time the data changes.

Notes

WirePrint’s bottle takes about two hours as a full print and fourteen minutes as a wireframe preview. Even the fast version is far from interactive.

Motivation

Perception without sensation

  • When vision and the body disagree, vision usually wins.
  • Seeing a rubber hand stroked shifts where people feel their own hand.
  • Pseudo-haptics uses this. Slow the cursor down and people feel friction, weight or a bump, with an ordinary mouse.

The hand’s sensation does not change. What people perceive does.

Rock & Victor 1964 · Botvinick & Cohen 1998 · Ernst & Banks 2002 · Lécuyer et al. 2004 · Lécuyer 2009

Notes

This is visuo-proprioceptive conflict. Psychology has explained it for decades. What is new is asking what it means for visualization, a field built on the assumption that perception follows the stimulus.

Motivation

An apparent paradox

The design goal

Lower cognitive load

Visualization moves work from thinking to seeing.

The capability

Load vision more

Pseudo-haptics adds a touch channel by putting more work on the visual system, the sense we usually add touch to relieve.

  • Separate senses usually draw on separate resources, which is why touch normally helps (Wickens 2008).
  • Pseudo-haptic touch arrives through vision, so it may not get that benefit.
  • But perceptual load is not cognitive load (Lavie et al. 2004). Extra seeing does not have to mean extra thinking.
Everyday example

Subtitles

Many viewers prefer reading subtitles to hearing a dub. They take on more visual work to avoid a mismatch between lips and voice, and to keep the original.

Notes

It only looks like a paradox from visualization’s point of view. Whether the extra load is perceptual and cheap, or cognitive and costly, is an open question this tool lets us study.

Motivation

Fidelity against speed

Pseudo-haptics is a low-fidelity version of the touch that fabrication provides. It renders instantly, changes with the data, and needs nothing but a mouse.

Shape displays · Follmer et al. 2013, Abtahi & Follmer 2018 · fast previews · Mueller et al. 2014

Notes

Same move as WirePrint, trading fidelity for speed so designers can iterate, but pushed all the way to no object at all.

The project

A prototyping tool for felt data

A browser tool where designers map data to a feel, test whether it comes across without vision, and decide what is worth fabricating.

Understand

What touch can carry

Measure which felt differences people tell apart, and turn them into design requirements such as tactile palettes, before anything is printed.

Realize

A new display medium

The cursor itself becomes a way to display data through touch on a common computer.

Users

  • Visualization and physicalization designers and researchers

Their tasks

  • Map an attribute to a feel and tune it
  • Test it with the visuals or the cursor hidden
  • Simplify the data to what is worth feeling
  • Compare the same data on screen and in print

Course direction · new visual analytics approach, and advanced analysis environments (multimodal interface)

Technical approach

From data to a feel

Computation

Persistent homology

Each peak is ranked by how far it rises above the saddle that joins it to a higher one. Peaks below a threshold are flattened, so only significant structure is felt.

Feel

Pointer lock and cursor gain

The browser locks the pointer and reads raw movement. Each step moves the cursor by hand movement × gain, and the gain comes from the data.

Interaction

Sculpt and replay

Sandbox lets people reshape the surface. Every brush step is recorded, so where they worked and what changed can be reviewed and replayed.

Edelsbrunner, Letscher & Zomorodian 2002 · Lécuyer 2009 · Samad et al. 2019

Expected results and evaluation

Evaluating a tool without a user study

Demonstration

What it can express

One set of channels and controls produces every prototype, from heatmap friction to network snapping to terrain.

Technical checks

Does the feel match the data?

Scripted cursor sweeps compare the felt signal with the true values. Replay must rebuild a session exactly.

Usage scenarios

Herman et al.’s tasks

Walkthroughs of tracing a path downhill, comparing two heights and finding extremes, felt on Maunga Whau.

Expected results

  • A working tool with three main prototypes
  • Evidence that felt encodings follow the data
  • A set of design considerations for felt encodings

Out of scope for now

  • A study with participants, so no human-subjects review is needed
  • Whether people read data accurately by feel, which is the main future study

Evaluation strategies for toolkits · Ledo et al. 2018
