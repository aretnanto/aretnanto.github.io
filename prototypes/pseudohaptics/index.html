Pseudo-haptics for data visualization

The cursor is scaled by g = 1 − k·v, where v is a data value scaled to 0–1, so the pointer feels sticky or slippery over data. CSC 596 interim demo.

Use case 1

Rank several friction settings from lightest to heaviest, with the cursor shown and hidden. People can tell friction levels apart by feel alone, and the visible cursor changes how heavy it seems.

Open C/D exploration

Use case 2

Colour already shows a value, and friction adds how much of each cell is filled in or how few reads back it (World Bank, gene expression). People find the unreliable cells faster and more accurately with friction than with colour alone.

World BankGene expression

Use case 3

Drag items into a bowl with tongs, where heavier items drag slower. Colour is food type, and size is not tied to weight. People hit about 12 oz at the lowest price more accurately when they feel the weight than when they only see size and colour.

Open mala tang game

Use case 4

Feel a dataset's surface with friction, tune the height mapping, then export an STL to print. Feeling the printed height on screen first lets people choose a mapping with fewer reprints.
